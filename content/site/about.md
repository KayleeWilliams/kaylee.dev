I'm a founding engineer at [Inth](https://inth.com) (YC P26) and co-author of [c15t](https://c15t.com), the open-source consent layer.

Consent tooling is usually the worst code on a page: a third-party script bolted on the week before launch, dragging down performance and ignoring your design system. I don't think it has to be. Compliance should be something you ship — in your bundle, in your components, reviewed like the rest of your code. That's the case I made on stage at the Next.js Conf London watch party, and it's the idea c15t is built on. At 1,800+ GitHub stars and 190K+ downloads a month, I'm clearly not the only one tired of the bolt-on model.

At Inth, I'm carrying that idea across the rest of the compliance stack: [DSAR](https://dsar-sdk.com) makes data-rights requests programmatic, [Cookiebench](https://cookiebench.com) measures what consent tooling actually costs, and [Leadtype](https://leadtype.dev) builds docs that agents can read. Different surfaces, same thesis: compliance belongs in code.
